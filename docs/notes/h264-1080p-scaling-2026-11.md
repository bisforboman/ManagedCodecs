# H.264 from 480p to 1080p, against FFmpeg (2026-11-10)

A measurement, no code change. Issue #402 reported decoding 50 times slower at 1080p than at 480p in a Godot run; that
figure came from a Debug build and from how `PlaybackStatistics.DecodeMilliseconds` counts under frame threading (the
issue's comments). The question here: in Release, on one thread, does any stage of ours grow faster with the frame
size than FFmpeg's does?

**Answer: no.** From 480p to 1080p our time grows 5.3 times and FFmpeg's 4.6 times, for 5.04 times the macroblocks.
The difference is not in how any stage scales. Stage by stage our growth matches FFmpeg's, except the output copy,
which FFmpeg does not have. If each of our stages grew by FFmpeg's factor for it (the copy by ours), our 1080p frame
would take 12.54 ms; it takes 12.6. What differs is where our time is: more of it is in inter prediction (26% at
480p, FFmpeg 20%). That stage grows 6.4 times in both decoders, while its instructions grow 4.8 times: reference reads
leave the caches at 1080p (a 1080p picture is about 3 MB). The output copy grows 9.5 times for the same reason. So the
way to close the gap at 1080p is the one at every size, inter prediction's cost per macroblock (3.2 times FFmpeg's
time here, +2.8 ms a 1080p frame). Nothing is specific to large frames.

## Method

As `docs/notes/h264-stages-2026-11.md`, with `tools/h264-stages.py` and its commands, on main at a7b8e52:

- Machine: the 4-core cloud VM. .NET 10, FFmpeg 6.1.1 (Ubuntu's, symbols from `libavcodec60-dbgsym`, its file name
  without the version's epoch, `6.1.1-3ubuntu5`, under ddebs.ubuntu.com/pool/universe/f/ffmpeg/; copied into
  /usr/lib/debug so that perf finds them).
- Streams: the Sintel trailer from download.blender.org, `sintel_trailer-480p.mp4` (854x480, 538 kb/s) and
  `-1080p.mp4` (1920x1080, 2.1 Mb/s): libx264 High, CABAC, B pictures, 1253 frames each. The stage tables use the first
  360 frames of each (`ffmpeg -frames:v 360 -an -c copy`), so that both sizes show the same scenes.
- Wall time: mcdec's last loop, three runs, the sizes alternating. FFmpeg's user time with `-threads 1`
  (`-benchmark`).
- Instructions: callgrind on a Native AOT mcdec, loops 2 minus loops 1.

## Whole trailers, one thread

| | ours, a loop | FFmpeg, user time |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 854x480 | 2.74–2.84 s | 1.20–1.27 s |
| 1920x1080 | 14.8–15.7 s | 5.59–5.79 s |
| 1080p / 480p | 5.3–5.6 | 4.5–4.8 |

Ours includes the AAC track (the same at both sizes, a small share), so its video alone grows slightly more.

## By stage, first 360 frames

Ours: 2.38 ms a frame at 480p, 12.6 at 1080p (5.3 times); 20.6 M instructions a frame against 100.3 M (4.87 times).
FFmpeg: 1.01 ms against 4.69 (4.6 times). µs a frame, the stage's perf share times the wall time.

| Stage | Ours 480p | Ours 1080p | Growth | FFmpeg 480p | FFmpeg 1080p | Growth | Our instr. growth |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Residual parsing | 174 | 781 | 4.5 | 63 | 305 | 4.9 | 4.8 |
| Header syntax (mb_type, mvd, ref, cbp) | 240 | 1159 | 4.8 | 224 | 1083 | 4.8 | 4.8 |
| MV prediction, neighbours, per-MB bookkeeping | 374 | 1991 | 5.3 | 68 | 385 | 5.7 | 4.8 |
| Inter prediction | 626 | 4032 | 6.4 | 198 | 1276 | 6.4 | 4.8 |
| Transform and residual add | 126 | 592 | 4.7 | 23 | 98 | 4.2 | 4.9 |
| Intra prediction | 131 | 731 | 5.6 | 26 | 145 | 5.5 | 5.8 |
| Deblocking: bS and per-edge QP | 152 | 680 | 4.5 | 36 | 197 | 5.4 | 4.8 |
| Deblocking: edge filters | 267 | 1348 | 5.1 | 177 | 900 | 5.1 | 5.2 |
| Per-picture setup and clears | 48 | 290 | 6.1 | 10 | 19 | 1.9 | 4.8 |
| Output copy | 57 | 542 | 9.5 | – | – | – | 2.3 |
| NAL unescaping, slice headers | 21 | 63 | 2.9 | 10 | 33 | 3.3 | 4.8 |
| Other (runtime, kernel, container) | 164 | 378 | 2.3 | 175 | 249 | 1.4 | 4.4 |

Readings:

- **Inter prediction** grows 6.4 times in both decoders for 4.8 times our instructions, so its time per instruction
  rises by a third: memory, not more work. On the whole trailers `PredictChromaPair` and `PredictLuma` grew 7.1 and
  6.6 times, and `PredictFromTwo` (bi-prediction, mostly under B_Skip and direct macroblocks by perf's call chains)
  13.7 times, against 5.04 times the macroblocks; the stage as a whole grew 6.9 times there (not split into
  instructions: callgrind ran only on the 360 frames).
- **Output copy** (`H264Decoder.Crop`, `SpanHelpers.Memmove`): 2.4% of a 480p frame, 4.3% of a 1080p one, at about
  10 GB/s on a 0.6 MB picture still in cache and 6 GB/s on a 3 MB one that is not.
  `docs/decisions/2026-11-06-h264-output-copy.md` has the options. Its option 2, borrowed frames that live until
  `Dispose`, would let Playback skip the copy: it decodes with frame threads, where `BorrowFrames` is not supported.
- **Per-picture clears** grow 6.1 times for 4.8 times the instructions, FFmpeg's 1.9 times: `ResetInter` clears the
  picture's motion vector and reference arrays whole when it begins, and at 1080p they no longer fit the caches. 2.3%
  of a 1080p frame.
- **Other** grows least in both: the fixed costs per frame and per packet (demuxing, the runtime) don't grow with the
  size.

## After the inter prediction PRs (2026-11-11)

Four bit-exact changes to inter prediction's kernels followed this note, each measured on the first 360 frames of the
1080p trailer: the chroma filter's row helper inlined (#475), weighted prediction by `pmaddwd` and saturating packs
(#479), the luma 6-tap filter across by `pmaddubsw` (#482) and the chroma row loop by width type and one pack (#487).
Two tries gained nothing and were dropped: prefetching list 1's reference rows, and caching implicit B weights per
slice (in #475's description).

Main before them (bab2e25) against main after (4082f5d), the same 360 frames:

| | 480p | 1080p |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Instructions a frame, before → after (callgrind, Native AOT) | 20.58 → 19.30 M (-6.2%) | 100.27 → 94.01 M (-6.2%) |
| Inter prediction alone | 6.45 → 5.16 M (-20%) | 30.99 → 24.73 M (-20%) |
| Wall time a frame, mcdec's last loop, six pairs alternating | 1.76–2.33 → 1.85–2.15 ms (within noise) | 11.5–13.1 → 10.9–11.9 ms (median ratio about 0.93) |

No other stage moved. Inter prediction now runs 2.8 times FFmpeg's instructions at 1080p (24.7 against 8.8 M), 3.5
times before; the whole decoder 2.56 times (94.0 against 36.7 M), 2.73 before. The 480p wall times on this VM spread
by more than the change; its instruction count fell as much as 1080p's. Kernel by kernel against FFmpeg (1080p, M
instructions a frame, before these PRs): luma filters and averages 11.3 against 2.7, weighting 4.7 against 0.8,
chroma 6.0 against 1.1. The chroma row loop is now 15 instructions, near FFmpeg's; what chroma has left at 1080p is
reference reads and per-block setup. What remains largest by instructions: motion vector prediction and per-macroblock
bookkeeping (16.3 against 4.6 M), then the edge filters (15.4 against 10.4) and the luma kernels' vertical and
quarter-sample passes.
