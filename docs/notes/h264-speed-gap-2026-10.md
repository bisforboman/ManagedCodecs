# H.264: where the remaining gap to FFmpeg comes from (2026-10-27)

A measurement, no code change. The question: the H.264 decoder takes 2 to 3 times FFmpeg's single-threaded time on
every stream class, after rounds of fixing one hot spot at a time flattened the profile (README, Open). Is there one
mechanism behind it, and what should change next?

**Answer.** The gap is the number of instructions executed: 3.6 to 4.2 times FFmpeg's, on all three streams measured,
while each of our instructions takes a little less time than FFmpeg's (on the 720p streams we run at 1.2 to 1.3 times
its instruction rate). Not memory: simulated cache misses per frame are the same as FFmpeg's or fewer. Not bounds
checks, write barriers, generic instantiation, interface dispatch or call overhead as such (each measured below). The
extra instructions come from how the work is cut up: per sample, per row, per 4x4 block, per edge and per partition,
each call working out again what is constant for the macroblock (its position, by division; its neighbours, by
coordinate lookups; its QPs; the block width, by branches in every row store). FFmpeg does that work once per
macroblock (one neighbour cache, filled once) and calls fixed-size kernels. So no single function dominates: every
stage executes more than FFmpeg's, the most where its work is cut into the smallest steps. The proposed first PR (end
of this note) applies the per-macroblock approach to inter prediction's per-partition path, the stage with the most
excess instructions and no open change in its files.

## Method

Machine: the 4-core cloud VM (Intel Xeon at 2.8 GHz, no SMT, 1 MiB L2 per core, 33 MiB L3; no hardware performance
counters, so `perf stat -e cache-misses` is not available). .NET 10.0.113 (runtime 10.0.13 under valgrind), FFmpeg
6.1.1 (Ubuntu's, with its `libavcodec60-dbgsym` symbols from ddebs.ubuntu.com so its profile resolves to functions).
Streams:

- `h264-bench-720p.mp4`: Constrained Baseline, CAVLC, one I and 49 P pictures, 1280x720.
- A 720p libx264 default stream (High, CABAC, B pictures, 8x8 transform), made from the same source as the bench sample:
  `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i "testsrc2=size=1280x720:rate=25,scroll=h=0.005,noise=alls=6:allf=t" -t 2 -c:v libx264
  -pix_fmt yuv420p` (not committed; README's 720p classes said the same of the others).
- `h264-high-default.mp4`: libx264's High defaults at 320x240.

Tools, each for what it can and cannot see (CLAUDE.md, Timing):

1. **Wall time**: `tools/compare.sh` (two runs each; 20, 10 and 200 loops).
2. **Where the time goes**: perf (`-e cpu-clock -F 4000`, `DOTNET_PerfMapEnabled=1 DOTNET_EnableWriteXorExecute=0
   DOTNET_TieredCompilation=0`), flat by function; and with `-g` (frame pointers work for JIT code) and
   `DOTNET_ReadyToRun=0` (so that `SpanHelpers.Memmove` and the other CoreLib helpers have names), each sample assigned
   to a stage by the first frame from the leaf that names one (`DeblockRows` and below: deblocking; `ReadResidual`
   and below: residual parsing; and so on). FFmpeg is assigned by its leaf function; its asm's local labels
   (`..@3485.branch_instr`) are resolved to their functions through the debug symbols.
3. **How many instructions**: callgrind on a **Native AOT build of mcdec** (`dotnet publish tools/ManagedCodecs.Cli -c
   Release -r linux-x64 -p:PublishAot=true -p:IlcInstructionSet=x86-64-v3`). This sidesteps CLAUDE.md's warning that
   the JIT falls back to MinOpts under valgrind: AOT code is compiled before valgrind sees it, and its speed is the
   JIT build's (7.79 against 7.6 to 8.0 ms a frame on the 720p bench sample). Loops N minus loops 1 gives the steady
   state. The AOT compiler inlines a little differently from the JIT (`AddClipped` and `Neighbor` are calls there), so
   function-level counts differ; stage totals do not depend on it. `--dump-instr=yes` with `objdump -d` of each binary
   classifies the executed instructions (vector, divisions, calls, branches).
4. **Memory**: cachegrind with a 48 KiB L1 and a 1 MiB last level (the per-core L2). For ours this runs the JIT's
   MinOpts code, which adds stack traffic (L1 hits) but reads and writes the pictures and tables as optimized code does;
   the miss counts are what matters.

Read across the three: the edge filters and residual parsing, each one tight kernel, run at 2.1 to 3.1 times FFmpeg's

## What was ruled out, and how

- **Memory traffic.** Cachegrind, steady state on `h264-bench-720p.mp4`, per frame: L1 data misses 130 k (ours)
  against 149 k (FFmpeg), last-level misses 75 k against 81 k. The misses are the same; we do not touch more memory.
  They do cost us: in the motion compensation kernels, most samples land on the first load of each reference row (by
  instruction, from perf samples matched with the JIT's listing: `CopyInside` 80%, `HalfHorizontal` and `HalfVertical`
  50%, `Bilinear` 43%), about 10% of the decoder's time waiting on reference reads. FFmpeg hides some of it with
  software prefetch (its `ff_prefetch` is 1.8% of its profile): it prefetches the reference area four macroblocks
  ahead. A scratch copy doing the same for luma and chroma (`Sse.Prefetch0`, four luma and two chroma rows a
  macroblock) took the kernels' samples down by about 3,400 of 125,000 (2.7%) but cost 1,900 itself (1.5%; not
  traced further), net -1.3%; wall clock -1.6% median of 6 interleaved pairs, spread ±5%. Worth
  folding into the per-partition rewrite below with its address arithmetic made cheap, not alone.
- **Bounds checks.** Counted in the JIT's listings of the hot methods (jumps to a `RNGCHKFAIL` block): none in the
  motion compensation filters and copies or the deblocking filter's vector code; 15 in `InverseTransformAdd`, 18 in
  `ReadResidualBlock`, 26 in `PredictPartition` (its jagged `_refPic[list][part]`, `_mvX[list][block]` reads). They
  are part of the per-call work below, not a cost of their own: the methods carrying most of them are small shares.
- **Call overhead as such.** Samples on function entries, push/pop/ret and the instruction after a call: 10.3% of ours
  against 8.3% of FFmpeg's, measured the same way (FFmpeg's from `objdump` of libavcodec). The share is about the same;
  what differs is how much work there is in all.
- **Write barriers**: 0.23 M instructions a frame, from storing a `Picture` reference per partition (`StoreMotion`;
  0.6% of the time). **Interface dispatch**: none per macroblock. **Generic instantiation**: `PictureDecoder<byte>` is a
  value-type instantiation; no hot method has a runtime generic lookup (`CORINFO_HELP_RUNTIMEHANDLE_*`), and the
  `typeof(TPixel)` branches fold. **GC**: allocation clearing is 0.24 M instructions a frame.
- **Divisions**: 65 k integer divisions a frame (18 a macroblock), all `mbAddr / _widthInMbs` or `% _widthInMbs`:
  `DeblockRows` 30 k, `LumaPlace` 19 k, `PartitionPlace` 8 k, `PredictPartition` 4 k, `MacroblockDone` 4 k. At 12 to 20
  cycles each that is at most 3.5 to 6% of a 720p frame if none overlapped; the functions holding them are 8% of the
  profile, most of it other work. A
  real but small item, best removed with the code around it.

What the executed instructions are (callgrind with `--dump-instr`, the 73% of ours and 86% of FFmpeg's whose
addresses `objdump` resolved): vector instructions are 16% of ours and 35% of FFmpeg's, compares and branches 23%
against 11%, calls, returns, pushes and pops 13% against 4%. In absolute numbers we execute more vector instructions
than FFmpeg (about 9 M a frame against 5 M), and about five times its scalar ones. The compares and branches are per
sample and per row: `AddClipped` (one call a residual sample, 155 k a frame, clamping by branches), `StorePartial`
(one a predicted row, 115 k a frame, a switch on the width each time), `FetchWindow`'s clamped path (282 calls, 4,200
instructions each), `FilterEdge` (36 k calls), `ChromaQp` (40 k calls, 11 a macroblock, the same few QPs again).

- 4 edits `PictureDecoder.Deblock.cs`, which has an open change by another session (as of this note), and so do the
  divisions in `DeblockRows`: wait for it.
- 2 is the largest ceiling on CABAC streams and the most work: it touches CAVLC and CABAC parsing, MV prediction, the
  direct modes and (with 4) the deblocking filter, and MBAFF frames need the pair-aware variant of the fill (Table
  6-4). Past attempts at caching neighbours in MBAFF (docs/conformance.md, Performance notes) added a cache on top of

- 5 is an API decision (who owns an output frame, and for how long), not only speed: for the owner.
- 6 was looked at before and left (each macroblock would clear its own state instead, about as many bytes).
- Together 1 to 4 reach 20 to 40% of the frame by their ceilings, which is the size of change the gap needs: the
  gap is spread over every stage, and only a change of approach in several of them closes a large part of it.

## Recommended first PR

**1, inter prediction's per-partition path**, for the owner to decide:

- It is the stage with the most excess instructions (+11 M a 720p frame, of +43 M in all) and the largest single
  share (24 to 30% of the time on the 720p streams), and its per-partition glue is about five times FFmpeg's.
- It is contained: `PictureDecoder.Inter.cs` and `InterPrediction*.cs` (neither has an open change; `VectorZip.cs` and
  `PictureDecoder.Deblock.cs` are untouched), and the filters themselves stay as they are, compared against the
  by-the-definition `PredictLumaReference` as today, with every conformance sample's MD5.
- It is measurable below the wall clock's noise: instructions a partition on the AOT build under callgrind (the
  target: under 500 from about 1,100), the changed functions' perf share before and after back to back, and
  `compare.yml` on hosted runners for the wall clock. Estimated 4 to 6% of a 720p frame, ceiling 8 to 10%.
- It is the pattern the rest needs (work out per macroblock what is constant for it, call fixed-size kernels), so 3
  and 2 follow it.

Then 2 as a design note first, 3, and 4 once the deblocking change has merged.

## A tool worth keeping

Callgrind on a Native AOT build counts the instructions of optimized code, which CLAUDE.md says valgrind cannot do with
the JIT (MinOpts). It repeats to the instruction, sees through inlining differences only at the function level, and
takes about a minute for 150 frames of 720p. CLAUDE.md's Timing section could name it next to the JIT one.

## Outcome of the first PR (2026-10-28)

What it did: each macroblock's column and row come from a table made with the decoder (`LumaPlace`, `ChromaPlace`
and `PartitionPlace` divide no more), the most common partition (one reference, no explicit weights, 8 bits, 4:2:0,
a frame macroblock of a frame) goes from an inlined `PredictPartition` straight to the luma and chroma kernels
(`PredictFromOne`) instead of through `PartitionPlace`, `Weighting` and three `PredictComponent` calls, and
`FetchWindow` copies each row's inside and repeats its edge samples rather than clamping sample by sample (checked
against the by-the-definition copy on random windows, `FetchWindow_MatchesReference`).

Instructions a frame (callgrind on the AOT build, as above): `h264-bench-720p.mp4` 59.08 to 56.14 M (-5.0%), the 720p
libx264 default stream 81.86 to 80.43 M (-1.7%: most of its partitions are bi-predicted, which keep the general
path), `h264-high-default.mp4` 6.98 to 6.89 M (-1.3%), `h264-mbaff-default.mp4` 11.02 to 10.94 M (-0.8%: the divisions
only). Perf on `h264-bench-720p.mp4`, three pairs back to back: the changed functions fell by about 9,800 samples and
the ones that took their work (`PredictFromOne`, its callers `DecodeSkip` and `DecodeInterMacroblock`, now inlining
the dispatch, and the kernels' entries) rose by 4,400, net about -5% of the decoder's samples; a third of
`PredictFromOne`'s own samples land on its prologue, the price of a call per partition that remains. Below the
estimate of 4 to 6% only on the streams with B pictures: bi-prediction and weighted prediction are the next part
of this path to give the same treatment.

## The rest of the per-partition rework (2026-10-28)

- **Prefetch.** The one-reference path asks the cache for the reference area four macroblocks ahead, four luma rows
  and two chroma rows of it a macroblock in turn (`InterPrediction.PrefetchAhead`, x64 only, nothing fetched outside
  the planes). The earlier scratch version cost 1.5% itself; this one about 0.9% (no clamping, no casts). Perf on
  `h264-bench-720p.mp4`, 4 pairs with the order alternating: the motion compensation kernels -10.8%, all H.264
  functions -2.2%, untouched functions +0.1% (no drift). (A first run with the same order in every pair read -8.5%;
  untouched functions moved 4% with it: drift, CLAUDE.md, Timing.)
- **Fixed-width rows.** The row copies and averages check their bounds once a block and choose the row store for the
  block's width once, through a type parameter (`CopyRows<TWidth>`, `AverageRows<TWidth>`), not in every row.
  Instructions a frame: `h264-bench-720p.mp4` 56.58 to 54.49 M (-3.7%), the 720p libx264 default stream 80.62 to
  76.60 M (-5.0%, its bi-predicted averages). Under the JIT, perf with the order alternating: neutral on the bench
  stream (inter prediction -1.2%, untouched functions +1.4%), inter prediction -7.3% on the CABAC stream with B
  pictures (untouched -2.4%), so about -5% of inter prediction and -2% of the decoding there.
- **One reference wait a macroblock**: not done. The waits (`WaitForRow`, `RowReady`) are about 0.55% of the samples,
  and most macroblocks are one partition (3,900 partitions to 3,400 inter macroblocks a 720p frame), so a wait a
  macroblock would save about an eighth of that, 0.07%: far below anything measurable, for a new threading wait to
  keep correct.

## Bi-predicted partitions (2026-10-28)

`PredictFromTwo` gives bi-predicted partitions (8 bits, 4:2:0, frame macroblocks of frames, no explicit weights) the
same direct path as `PredictFromOne`: both predictions into the scratch blocks, then the rounded average, or the
implicit weights (`weighted_bipred_idc` 2, libx264's default) where they differ from it. Instructions a frame: the 720p
libx264 default stream 76.67 to 76.00 M (-0.9%; what still takes the general path there is mostly P partitions with
explicit weights, libx264's default `weightp`), `h264-high-default.mp4` 6.53 to 6.46 M (-1.1%). Perf, four pairs with
the order alternating: inter prediction -2.7% on the 720p stream (untouched functions +1.2%: within the noise),
-8.3% on `h264-high-default.mp4`, where all H.264 functions fell 2.8% (untouched -0.9%). Its two reference waits are
mutations of their own; the list-0 one survived 3 runs of the delayed-thread tests and failed on the 4th, so both run
up to 10 times. Next on this path, by the same measure: explicit weights for one reference.

## Explicit weights (2026-10-28)

The direct paths take explicit weights too (`weighted_pred_flag`, libx264's default `weightp` in P slices): one
reference predicts into the scratch block and `WeightOne` writes the picture when the partition's weights are not the
default ones, straight into the picture when they are; two references take the table's weights through `Weighting`
as the general path does. Instructions a frame: the 720p libx264 default stream 76.01 to 75.66 M (-0.5%),
`h264-wp-default.mp4` 6.12 to 6.08 M (-0.75%). Perf on the 720p stream, four pairs with the order alternating: inter
prediction -4.4%, all H.264 functions -1.6%, untouched functions -0.1%. What remains on the general path for 8-bit
4:2:0 is field macroblocks and field pictures; deeper samples and 4:2:2 and 4:4:4 take it throughout.

## The residual add in vectors (2026-10-28)

For 8-bit samples, `InverseTransformAdd`, `InverseTransform8x8Add` and `AddDc` keep the rows' transform as it was and
do the columns' four columns at a time (vectors of four ints, the same integer operations), each output row added to
its samples and clipped in 32-bit lanes; `AddDc` adds one broadcast residual a row. Deeper samples keep the scalar
code (`...Scalar`), which random-input tests compare the vector one with (`InverseTransformAdd_MatchesScalar`,
`InverseTransform8x8Add_MatchesScalar`). Instructions a frame, 4x4 and DC: `h264-bench-720p.mp4` 54.60 to 50.31 M
(-7.9%; the AOT build calls `AddClipped` a sample, so this overstates what the JIT build gains), the 720p libx264
default stream 75.66 to 72.96 M (-3.6%), `h264-high-default.mp4` 6.42 to 6.22 M (-3.1%). Perf, the order
alternating: the transform and add functions' share of `h264-bench-720p.mp4` from 8.8 to 9.0% down to 6.8 to 7.0% in
every run, and the H.264 functions' samples over untouched functions' -1.4% (that machine's speed moved 7% between
runs: normalizing by untouched functions takes it out); `h264-high-default.mp4` -1.4% (untouched -0.3%). The 8x8
transform's share of the 720p libx264 default stream from 1.77 to 1.06%. (The vector transforms reverted in #42 did
the rows and columns both in vectors and kept the add a sample at a time; this keeps the rows and changes the add.)

## Deblocking a macroblock at a time (2026-10-28)

The first part of the recommendation above: the work around the filters. For 8-bit 4:2:0 frame macroblocks
`FilterMacroblock420` takes a macroblock's edges in the order `FilterLumaEdges` and the chroma loop did, but reads each
edge's parameters (indexA's tC0 row, alpha and beta, by the average QP of its two sides) and Cb's and Cr's QP_C from
tables made once for the slice's offsets (`EdgeTables`, remade only when a slice's offsets differ), and calls the
vector filters (`FilterColumns`, `FilterRows`, `FilterChromaRows`) directly instead of through `FilterEdge` and
`FilterChromaEdge`, which work all of that out for each edge. Other sample types and formats, and machines without
vector hardware, keep the edge-by-edge path, which a random-input test compares the new one with
(`Macroblock420_MatchesEdgeByEdge`: blocky planes, strengths with and without the 8x8 transform's edges, QPs, offsets).

Instructions a frame (callgrind on the AOT build): `h264-bench-720p.mp4` 49.69 to 45.25 M (-8.9%), the 720p libx264
default stream 70.06 to 65.45 M (-6.6%), `h264-high-default.mp4` 6.02 to 5.63 M (-6.5%). Of that, the
macroblock-at-a-time calls were about 2.9 M on the 720p CAVLC stream (`FilterLumaEdges`, `FilterEdge`,
`FilterChromaEdge` gone, `DeblockRows` 1.78 to 0.84 M) and the tables 1.5 M (`ChromaParameters`, `ChromaQp` and the luma
parameters). Perf, the order alternating, H.264 functions over untouched
ones: -5.7%, -3.3% and -5.2%. The edge strengths (`MotionStrength`, `HasUniformMotion` and the rest: 3.6 M on the
CAVLC stream, against FFmpeg's 0.5) are the second part.

## Edge strengths (2026-10-28)

The second part: bS in frames, for the common case of motion from list 0 alone (P macroblocks, and B macroblocks
predicted from list 0 only).

- **Internal edges in vectors** (`OneListStrengths`): the 16 blocks' motion vectors as four vectors, each block's left
  and upper neighbour by a shuffle, the 4 (and limitY) thresholds, the coded blocks and the four partitions' reference
  pictures (only edge 2 crosses partitions) compared at once, and the vertical edges' strengths transposed into
  place. Checked against 8.7.2.1 block pair by block pair on random input (`OneListStrengths_MatchTheDefinition`, frame
  and field limits). Macroblocks with list 1 motion keep the segment-by-segment path.
- **Macroblock edges**: the intra test once an edge rather than once a segment, and for two one-list macroblocks the
  motion compared directly (`MotionStrength`'s first case without its tests of the lists).
- **P_Skip is uniform** (`HasUniformMotion`): one list 0 vector, and list 1 as the picture's start cleared it.
- **8x8 transforms**: a macroblock's coded blocks found an 8x8 block at a time (4 calls, not 16).

Instructions a frame (callgrind on the AOT build), against main after the first part: `h264-bench-720p.mp4` 45.25 to
43.59 M (-3.7%), the 720p libx264 default stream 65.45 to 64.34 M (-1.7%), `h264-high-default.mp4` 5.63 to 5.50 M
(-2.4%), `h264-mbaff-default.mp4` 9.58 to 9.51 M (-0.7%). Perf, the order alternating, H.264 functions over untouched
ones: -2.6%, -1.4% and -1.7%. Both parts together: 49.69 to 43.59 M on the CAVLC stream (-12.3%) and 70.06 to 64.34 M on
the CABAC one (-8.2%). What is left of bS is mostly bi-predicted blocks (`MotionStrength`'s other cases, about 1.6 M
on the CABAC stream with B pictures), which a vector version of the two-list rule could take next.

## Edge strengths of bi-predicted blocks (2026-10-28)

What an edge between two blocks compares follows from their partitions' reference pictures alone (`MotionRule`: bS 1
whatever the motion, or which of the four list pairings to test, and whether one or both must be far), so a
macroblock's internal edges take one rule per pair of partitions (four with themselves, two that edge 2 crosses) and
`TwoListStrengths` computes the four distances for every block in vectors and selects by each lane's rule. Checked
against 8.7.2.1 block pair by block pair on random references (one or two pictures, the same picture in both lists,
swapped lists) and motion (`TwoListStrengths_MatchTheDefinition`). Macroblock edges take two rules an edge (segments 0
and 1, 2 and 3, each lie between one pair of partitions) instead of `MotionStrength` working the references out per
segment.

Instructions a frame (callgrind on the AOT build): the 720p libx264 default stream 64.19 to 63.68 M (-0.8%),
`h264-high-default.mp4` 5.50 to 5.42 M (-1.3%); none on the P-only bench stream. Perf, the order alternating: -0.3% and
-0.2%, below its noise, as expected of a change this size. The first version of `TwoListStrengths` saved nothing: its
small helper functions were calls on the AOT build, as many instructions as the scalar code they replaced; inlined, it
saves a quarter of a percent, and the macroblock edges the rest. Of bS on the CABAC stream with B pictures, about 2.4 M
a frame is left (`MacroblockEdgeStrengths`, `DeblockRows`' per-macroblock tests, `HasUniformMotion`), against FFmpeg's
0.8 M for all of it.

## CABAC's ctxIdxInc (2026-10-28)

Most of the neighbours that coded_block_flag, coded_block_pattern's luma bins and mvd look at are inside the
macroblock (9 of a 4x4 luma block's 16 pairs, the second block of each chroma AC row and column, half the cbp bins):
`CodedBlockFlagInc`, `CabacCbp` and `CabacMvd` now read those from the macroblock's own counts, bits and |mvd| directly,
and go through `Neighbor` and the per-category rules (`CodedBlockFlagCond`) only across a macroblock edge.
`StoreAbsMvd` writes a partition's row of |mvd| in one store instead of two indexed stores a block. No new arithmetic:
the conformance samples (every CABAC one, MBAFF and field pictures included) check it.

Instructions a frame (callgrind on the AOT build): the 720p libx264 default stream 63.68 to 62.41 M (-2.0%),
`h264-high-default.mp4` 5.42 to 5.34 M (-1.5%), `h264-mbaff-default.mp4` 9.42 to 9.30 M (-1.2%). Perf, the order
alternating, H.264 functions over untouched ones: -1.6% and -1.9%.

## Motion bookkeeping: not kept (2026-10-28)

The motion vector prediction and storage of frames (`MotionNeighbor` 1.7 to 2.9 M instructions a frame on the 720p
streams, `StoreMotion` 1.2 to 1.6 M) did not give way to local changes:

- **Neighbours inside the macroblock read directly** (as CABAC's contexts now are): `MotionNeighbor`'s instructions did
  not move. Its lookups are almost all across a macroblock edge (a 16x16 partition's A, B and C all are), and so are
  spatial direct's.
- **P_Skip's A and B passed on to `PredictMv`** rather than found again: the second overload's call and two `Motion`
  values passed by value cost more than the two lookups (+0.8% on the CABAC stream, all variants together).
- **`MotionNeighbor` without the tuple** (the location modulo 16 worked out directly in frames, `>> 2` for `/ 4`): under
  0.1%.
- **`StoreMotion` a partition row in one store**: through spans and `MemoryMarshal.Write` slower (each slice checked);
  through one bounds check a macroblock and `Unsafe` writes -0.7% of the AOT build's instructions on both 720p streams,
  but nothing under the JIT (perf, the order alternating: +0.3% and 0.0%), whose `Span.Fill` of the original is already
  cheap there. Not worth unchecked writes; the branch `claude/motion-bookkeeping-experiment` keeps it.

About 123 instructions a `MotionNeighbor` call and 314 a `StoreMotion` call remain (23,000 and 5,100 calls a frame of
the CABAC stream), against FFmpeg's caches filled once a macroblock; the neighbour context that would replace them was
slower in frames (docs/decisions/2026-10-28-h264-neighbour-context.md).

## CABAC's significance map without bounds checks (2026-10-28)

`Cabac.ResidualBlock` checked three spans for every bin of the significance map (the significant and last
ctxIdxInc tables and the positions found) and `Decide` the LPS range table for every bin of every syntax element. The
increments are now checked against maxNumCoeff once a block and read through references, as the contexts already
were; the range table's index is under 256 by construction (pStateIdx under 64). Instructions a frame (callgrind on the
AOT build): the 720p libx264 default stream 62.40 to 62.15 M (-0.4%), `h264-high-default.mp4` 5.34 M (no change).
Under the JIT, which kept more of the checks: `ResidualBlock`'s samples over untouched functions -6.7% and -5.1%, all
H.264 functions -1.6% and -1.0% (perf, the order alternating). The kernel's arithmetic stays FFmpeg's equal in kind (one
decision per bin, the same renormalization); what is left of its 2.2 times FFmpeg's instructions is the branches of
`Decide`, which FFmpeg's assembly replaces with conditional moves.
