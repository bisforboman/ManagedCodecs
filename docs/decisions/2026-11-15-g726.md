# G.726 ADPCM, in .wav files and over RTSP (2026-11-15)

Issue #527 lists G.726 among the audio a camera may send that `LivePlayer` skips (`SkippedAudioCodec`). Built on the
owner's choice from #527's list (2026-11-15); the one decision it needed is below.

## What G.726 is

- ITU-T G.726 (1990): adaptive differential PCM of 8 kHz speech at 16, 24, 32 or 40 kbit/s, a code of 2 to 5 bits a
  sample. It took in G.721 (32 kbit/s) and G.723 (24 and 40). Everything is integer arithmetic the Recommendation gives
  block by block: an inverse adaptive quantizer, a scale factor that adapts fast and slow, a speed control with a tone
  and transition detector, and a predictor of two poles and six zeros, its values in a small floating-point form.
- **Cameras:** from memory, not checked: ONVIF lists it beside G.711 and AAC, and some cameras offer it. No camera was
  in reach.
- **Over RTP** (RFC 3551, 4.5.4): dynamic payload types named `G726-16` to `G726-40`, the codes packed from each
  octet's least significant bit; `AAL2-G726-*`, names registered beside them, pack from the most significant, as ATM's
  AAL2 does, and as FFmpeg's .wav muxer does. FFmpeg's RTP packetizer sends both.
- **In .wav:** the format tag 0x45, `wBitsPerSample` the bits a code, the codes from each byte's most significant bit
  (FFmpeg's muxer writes no extra bytes).

## FFmpeg is not the Recommendation

Every decoder here is checked against FFmpeg sample by sample (CLAUDE.md, Core idea). For G.726 that can't be done:
FFmpeg's adpcm_g726 is an implementation of its own, and its samples are not the Recommendation's.

Measured (2026-11-16), each .wav sample decoded by FFmpeg 6.1 and by the reference below, as 16-bit values:

| Sample | Samples that differ | Largest difference | FFmpeg's against the Recommendation's |
|---|---|---|---|
| `wav-g726-16` (FFmpeg's encoder, a tone) | 8397 of 10400 | 1396 | 24.9 dB |
| `wav-g726-24` | 3918 | 1348 | 26.8 dB |
| `wav-g726-32` | 734 | 460 | 38.1 dB |
| `wav-g726-40` | 10386 | 7176 | 7.3 dB |
| `wav-g726-itu-*` (the Recommendation's encoder, a loud sweep) | 1277 to 10364 | full scale | 4 to 7 dB |

Two checks say the reference, not FFmpeg, is the Recommendation:

- **ITU-T's digital test sequences** (G.726 Appendix II, as the ITU-T Software Tool Library distributes them in
  `src/g726/test_data` of github.com/openitu/STL): `tools/g726-reference.py verify` runs all 56 (every decoder sequence
  at each rate, normal, overload and code sequences, to A-law and to µ-law through the synchronous coding adjustment, and
  every encoder sequence), with 0 wrong samples or codes. The sequences aren't committed, as their licence is the ITU's.
- **Sun Microsystems' g72x code** (public domain, as SoX carries it; 24, 32 and 40 kbit/s), run by hand on FFmpeg's three
  tone files: the same samples as `G726Decoder`, every one.

## Decided

Asked with the multiple-choice prompt; the owner took the recommendation (2026-11-16).

1. **Which G726Decoder follows. Decided: (a), the Recommendation.**
   - **(a) The Recommendation** (recommended). As cameras' encoders, which are built to it, mean their codes. CI checks
     `G726Decoder` against `tools/g726-reference.py` on every sample; that tool is proven on Appendix II by hand. A
     40 kbit/s file FFmpeg wrote sounds worse here than in FFmpeg, whose encoder and decoder agree with each other.
   - (b) FFmpeg, bit for bit, as every other decoder here. A camera's 40 kbit/s sound then decodes as FFmpeg decodes it.
   - (c) Both, by a public switch on `G726Decoder`.
   - (d) Leave G.726 out.

## Built

- **`G726Decoder`** (Core, public, as the other small decoders): one channel, 2 to 5 bits a code (the stream's
  CodecPrivate byte; 4 when it has none), its state carried from packet to packet, each packet's codes from its first
  byte. Its output is the reconstructed signal limited to 14 bits, over 8192: the Recommendation's uniform PCM
  interface. **`CodecIds.AdpcmG726`** (from each byte's most significant bit) and **`CodecIds.AdpcmG726Le`** (from the
  least), as FFmpeg names them.
- **.wav:** the format tag 0x45 in mono at 2 to 5 bits, each packet's duration its codes.
- **RTSP:** `G726-16` to `G726-40` and `AAL2-G726-*`, one channel, as an audio track that plays (`RtspSource.Audio`):
  a packet's payload is its codes, decoded on from the last packet.
- **`tools/g726-reference.py`**: the Recommendation in plain Python integers. `decode` writes the samples' references
  (`name.g726.f32`), `encode` makes the `wav-g726-itu` samples (FFmpeg's encoder isn't the Recommendation's either),
  `repack` turns one packing into the other, and `verify` runs Appendix II.
- **Samples** (make-samples.sh), at each rate: `wav-g726-N` (FFmpeg's encoder on a tone), `wav-g726-itu-N` (the
  Recommendation's encoder on a loud sweep with quiet stretches and steps, reaching the tone and transition detector,
  the poles' limits and the output's), `wav-g726-codes-N` (random codes, as no encoder sends them: the 16-bit wraps of
  the predictor's sums); and FFmpeg's RTP packetizer on the `itu` codes as `G726-N` and `AAL2-G726-N`.

## Checks

- **`G726DecoderTests`**: every .wav sample decoded exactly as the reference decodes it, every sample and no more; RTP's
  packing decoded alike; packets cut anywhere between codes give the same samples; a packet's bits after its last
  whole code are ignored; the WAVE format tag; what the decoder refuses.
- **`RtpG726Tests`**: FFmpeg's RTP recordings give the file's codes back in each packing; the SDP's names; through
  `LivePlayer`, every chunk taken is `G726Decoder`'s for the packets in order, over the RTSP connection and over UDP.
- **Not checked:** a camera's G.726 (none in reach), and GStreamer's `rtpg726pay` (its packing options weren't tried).
  Appendix II runs by hand, not in CI.
