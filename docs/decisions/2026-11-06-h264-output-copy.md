# H.264: the output copy (2026-11-06)

**Decided, 2026-11-06: option 1 first, measured; it gained too little and was dropped** (Outcome, at the end).

## The problem

Every decoded H.264 frame is copied out of the decoder's picture into a frame of the caller's (`H264Decoder.Crop`):
2.6, 2.8 and 1.8% of the single-threaded time on the three streams of `docs/notes/h264-stages-2026-11.md` (first
given there as 4.3 to 5.0%, a misattribution since corrected), a stage FFmpeg does not have, for almost no instructions (0.17 M a 720p frame). It is memory traffic: by the time
a picture is output, its 1.4 MB at 720p (more with its padding) has left the caches, so the copy reads it back from
memory and writes as much again. README's numbers for `BorrowFrames`, which skips it: about 5% of the decoding time at
720p, 1% at 320x240.

## What callers rely on now

- A returned frame **owns its samples**: the planes are the caller's to read, keep and write (README, Decoding a file;
  `docs/decisions/2026-10-26-api-before-0.1.0.md`, Raw arrays). Overlays, in-place colour conversion and pooling
  frames of one's own all work.
- Its planes are **tight in practice**: a pooled copy has offset 0 and a stride of the plane's width, so code that
  reads `Planes[0]` as width x height samples works, although the API only promises `Strides` and `Offsets`.
- `Dispose` is optional: a frame not disposed is collected, and only its planes' reuse is lost.
- `BorrowFrames = true` gives frames that point into the decoder's pictures (macroblock-aligned strides, offsets for
  the crop): valid only until the next `Decode` or `Flush`, not to be written, 8-bit only (deeper samples are kept as
  `ushort`), and not with `Threads` > 1. mcdec uses it under `--borrow-frames`.

## Options

1. **Copy while the rows are hot (no API change).** The picture's output frame is rented when the picture begins, and
   each macroblock row is copied into it as it becomes final (one row after it is deblocked, when frame threading
   reports it), while it is still in the L2 cache, instead of the whole picture once it is output. Callers see
   nothing change. The copy's instructions stay; what should go is most of the memory traffic, so the gain is
   somewhere between nothing and the whole 2 to 3%, and only a measurement says where (a rough version first, timed
   by perf in alternating pairs as usual). Costs: a frame is rented earlier, per picture, also for pictures that are
   never output (rare: `no_output_of_prior_pics_flag`, concealment drops), and the copy moves into the deblocking
   loop (MBAFF and field pictures by pair rows, a frame's two fields by field).
2. **Borrowed frames that live until `Dispose` (an addition, minor version).** `BorrowFrames` frames would keep their
   picture from being reused until they are disposed or collected, rather than only until the next call, and so work
   with `Threads` > 1. The decoder allocates a new picture when its pooled ones are all held. The promise widens (a
   frame valid for longer), so code written for today's `BorrowFrames` keeps working; the frames are still not to be
   written, and still 8-bit only. Only callers who opt in gain, which mcdec, the players (mcplay, the Godot and Unity
   samples) and the thumbnails would.
3. **Zero copy by default (a breaking change, minor version with a "Breaking changes" entry).** Option 2's frames for
   everyone. Frames would no longer be the caller's to write (a write would corrupt the pictures later ones predict
   from), and their planes would no longer be tight. Both breaks are silent: code that writes into a frame or reads
   `Planes[0]` as tight decodes garbage rather than failing. Every caller gains.

Option 1 can be combined with 2 (callers who borrow skip the copy, the rest get a cheaper one). Option 3 rules out 1.

## Recommendation

**1 first, measured; then 2 if you want the borrowing callers to gain the rest.** Option 1 is the only one that changes
no promise, and it says how much of the 2 to 3% is the cold reads (if most of it, 2 and 3 are not worth their API
cost). Option 3 trades a few percent for two silent breaks, against the API record's "each frame owns its samples":
not recommended.

## Decisions

Asked with the multiple-choice prompt (2026-11-06).

1. **Which way. Decided:** option 1 first, as a rough version timed under the JIT in alternating pairs; whether to add
   option 2 is decided on its result. Option 3 is not taken.

## Outcome

A rough version of option 1 (8-bit frames on one thread, without concealment: the output frame rented when the
picture began and each run of final rows copied into it from `Picture.ReportProgress`) passed every H.264 test. The
copy, inclusive by perf's call chains (`DOTNET_ReadyToRun=0`, three alternating pairs a stream), took 1.9 to 2.2% of
`h264-bench-720p.mp4`'s time against 2.5 to 2.6% for `Crop`, and 1.6 to 1.9% of the 720p libx264 default stream's
against 2.6 to 2.9%: the reads in cache save a fifth to a third of the copy, 0.5 to 1 point of the frame, and the
frame-level ratio (total over the motion compensation and deblocking kernels, six pairs) moved -0.4 and -0.1%.
The full version would also need frame threading's copy, field pictures, MBAFF pair rows, concealment and failed
pictures, for under 1%: not built. What remains is option 2, which removes the copy for callers who borrow.
