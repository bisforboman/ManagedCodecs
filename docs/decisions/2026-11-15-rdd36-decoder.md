# SMPTE RDD 36 decoder, slice 1: 4:2:2, progressive, 10 bits, from MOV

Issue #461, slice 1. The owner decided (2026-11-15, on the issue) that the decoders of #461 are built in this
repository, open, under its licence; the naming; and the order of the slices. This record is about how slice 1 is
built.

## Decisions

- **A package of its own**, `ManagedCodecs.Rdd36`, with `Rdd36Decoder : IVideoDecoder` and `CodecIds.Rdd36`
  ("RDD36"), named from the specification (the owner's decision: neutral IDs). In the meta-package and in the Unity
  build, as the other decoders are. `Mp4Demuxer` maps QuickTime's six sample entries ('apco', 'apcs', 'apcn', 'apch',
  'ap4h', 'ap4x') to the codec; the decoder needs nothing of the sample entry, since each frame's header says it all
  (RDD 36 leaves the file format out).
- **Written from the RDD 36:2022 PDF alone**, fetched on its own from pub.smpte.org (the 2022 folder serves
  `rdd36-2022.pdf` beside the zip with the reference decoder, which was never downloaded). Not committed.
- **Bit-exact with FFmpeg by measurement.** RDD 36 fixes no inverse transform: it qualifies one by its accuracy
  (Annex A, after IEEE 1180). To give FFmpeg's samples, the arithmetic was found from FFmpeg 6.1.1's output on
  streams written for the purpose, never from its source:
  - Frames of one slice whose quantized coefficients were chosen (written from the syntax tables, quantization weights
    of 2 and qScale 1 so that coefficient times 8 is the level times 2), decoded by `ffmpeg`, and each block's 64
    samples read back: DC sweeps, single coefficients at every position, random sparse and dense blocks (21,000).
  - The family `SimpleIdct` (MJPEG) belongs to fitted the sweeps and single coefficients at once, but left about 1.5%
    of dense blocks off by one at any precision and rounding. Blocks of one row or one column (about 29,000 more, a
    frame of 1024 blocks at a time) separate the passes; for each output sample of a column pass, linear programming
    on the measured samples bounded each constant to an interval about 2 wide at 2^16 scale. Every interval held four
    times cos(k pi / 16) sqrt(2) 2^14 rounded, but W3's held 4 * 19265 and not 4 * 19266, and W4's 4 * 2^14 (not
    2^14 - 1, as `SimpleIdct` has). With those the fit is exact on every measured block in range.
  - Coefficients beyond what pictures give (|level * weight * qScale| past about 2^14): FFmpeg's C and its x64 SIMD
    transform give different samples, so neither is "FFmpeg's"; the decoder wraps at 16 bits and that is its
    definition. Every sample's references are the C transform's (`-cpuflags 0`), which the SIMD one equals on all of
    them.
  - The arithmetic meets Annex A on its own (`Rdd36IdctTests`): peak error 0.106 (limit 0.15), mean square error at a
    pixel 0.00067 (0.002), overall 0.00065 (0.001), mean error at a pixel 0.00086 (0.0015), overall 0.00007
    (0.00015), at the worst of the six runs.
- **What RDD 36 leaves open, as FFmpeg does** (measured): the clamp 4..1019 (7.5.1 allows it or 0..1023);
  quantization_index 0 as 1 and 225 to 255 as 224 (reserved values).
- **Where the decoder follows RDD 36 rather than FFmpeg**:
  - A slice header longer than its syntax elements, and reserved bits that are set: 6.4 and 6.1.1 say to use the
    sizes and ignore the bits; FFmpeg refuses such frames.
  - Colour codes 0 and 2 are both unknown in RDD 36 (Tables 5 and 6); FFmpeg shows 0 as H.273's "reserved". The
    decoder takes the container's colour then, as for 2.
  - aspect_ratio_information gives the frames' aspect ratio where the container has none ('pasp'); FFmpeg ignores it.
    No sample has a frame header that says one without a 'pasp' (FFmpeg's muxer always writes one).
- **Samples**: FFmpeg's two encoders, prores_ks and prores_aw, at the four 4:2:2 profiles and in the cases they
  reach (docs/conformance.md lists them). What they never write comes from `tools/rdd36-rewrite.py`, which reads a
  stream down to its coefficients and writes it again with the feature (higher and reserved quantization indices,
  version 1, zero bytes, longer headers, colour in the frame header), so FFmpeg's decoder is still the reference.
  What FFmpeg refuses is covered by unit tests on frames written by the tests' own writer. No Apple-encoded file is
  used: none was found under a licence that allows it in a public repository.
- **No vector path yet**: speed is slice 5 (slice threading first). The decoder's shortcuts (rows of zeros, rows with
  only their first coefficient, blocks with only their first row) are tested against `Rdd36Idct.PutReference`, the
  plain two passes, on random blocks.

## Not in this slice

Interlaced frames, 4:4:4 and alpha, 12 bits (slice 2: the decoder refuses them with `NotSupportedException`);
decoding from `MxfDemuxer` (slice 3); thumbnails and `MediaPlayer` (they take H.264 and MJPEG only); speed.
