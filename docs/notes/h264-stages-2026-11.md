# H.264 against FFmpeg by stage, again (2026-11-06)

A measurement, no code change: `docs/notes/h264-speed-gap-2026-10.md`'s tables on main at 781805d,
after the speed PRs since (the per-partition inter path, CAVLC's 64-bit bit cache, CABAC's engine in registers and its
transitions as constant data, the 16-bit transforms and more; docs/conformance.md, H.264, Performance notes). The question:
where is the remaining gap now, and what ranks first?

**Answer.** On one thread mcdec now takes 2.0 to 2.2 times FFmpeg's time on all three streams (3.2 and 2.8 times on
the 720p ones in October), and executes 2.7 to 3.1 times its instructions (3.6 to 4.2). FFmpeg's counts did not move
(the same build, within 1% by stage), so the change is ours: 59.1 to 41.5 M instructions a 720p CAVLC frame, 81.9 to
59.9 a CABAC one. Per instruction we still run faster than FFmpeg (1.2 to 1.3 times its rate at 720p). By stage, the edge
filters and header syntax are now at or under FFmpeg's time (1.3 to 2.0 and 0.7 to 1.3 times); the furthest behind are the **transform and residual add**
(5.2 to 6.2 times FFmpeg's time, 5.0 to 7.2 times its instructions), **intra prediction**, **per-picture clears**
and the **output copy**, which FFmpeg does not do (4.3 to 5.0% of our time on every stream, almost no instructions: it
is memory traffic). In excess microseconds the largest are CABAC residual parsing (+1.1 ms a 720p frame), inter
prediction (+0.6 to +0.8 ms) and motion bookkeeping (+0.27 to +0.56 ms), each already worked on (Ranked, below).

## Method

As the October note, with `tools/h264-stages.py` (new; its docstring has the commands) doing the stage assignment:

- Machine: the 4-core cloud VM, no SMT, no hardware counters. .NET 10.0.113, FFmpeg 6.1.1 (Ubuntu's,
  `libavcodec60-dbgsym` from ddebs.ubuntu.com for its symbols: ffmpeg.org and GitHub's archives are not reachable from
  the cloud sandbox, so no FFmpeg of our own build).
- Streams: `h264-bench-720p.mp4` (Constrained Baseline, CAVLC, I and 49 P), a 720p libx264 default stream (High, CABAC,
  B, 8x8 transform; `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i "testsrc2=size=1280x720:rate=25,scroll=h=0.005,noise=alls=6:allf=t" -t 2
  -c:v libx264 -pix_fmt yuv420p`, not committed) and `h264-high-default.mp4` (320x240).
- Wall time: five runs each, the two decoders interleaved, medians. mcdec's is its last loop (40, 20 and 200 loops);
  FFmpeg's is its user time over all frames with `-stream_loop` (one open, so unlike October no reopening per loop is
  in it; that was a third of its 320x240 time then).
- Time by stage: perf with call graphs (`-g`, `DOTNET_ReadyToRun=0`), each mcdec sample given to the first frame from
  the leaf that names a stage; FFmpeg by its leaf (its asm keeps no frame pointers), local asm labels resolved through
  the debug symbols. FFmpeg's `ff_h264_filter_mb_fast` and `loop_filter` count as edge filters, as October's table
  had them (their own time is setup and the inlined `filter_mb_dir`).
- Instructions: callgrind, loops N minus loops 1, on a Native AOT mcdec (`-p:IlcInstructionSet=x86-64-v3`) and on
  FFmpeg, each function's own count assigned by the same rules. callgrind gives no call chains there, so mcdec's libc
  memset counts as the per-picture clears and memmove as the output copy (what perf's call chains show them to be);
  CoreLib's `SpanHelpers.Fill`, `Memmove` and `Clear` stay in "other" (about 1.2 M a 720p frame).

## Ranked: what is left

A change cannot gain more than its stage's share; the estimate takes the stage to about twice FFmpeg's time, as our
residual parsing and edge filters already are.

| # | Stage | Share (CAVLC / CABAC / 320x240) | Time ratio | Ceiling | Estimate | State |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 1 | Transform and residual add | 8.9 / 5.4 / 5.0% | 5.2 to 6.2 | 5 to 9% | 3 to 6% | The 4x4 and 8x8 transforms are in 16-bit lanes; the add and clip around them (`AddInterResidual`, `ReconstructChroma`, `AddDc`, chroma DC) is still per block and per sample in places: October's candidate 3, half done |
| 2 | Output copy | 4.3 / 4.4 / 5.0% | – | 4 to 5% | 4% | Not worked on: handing out the decoded picture (pooled, reference counted) is an API decision for the owner (October's candidate 5) |
| 3 | Intra prediction | 3.3 / 3.0 / 8.6% | 4 to 18 | 3 to 8% | 2 to 5% | Not worked on since October; most of the 320x240 stream's share |
| 4 | Inter prediction | 23.7 / 16.9 / 15.8% | 2.5 to 4.2 | large | 3 to 5% | The per-partition path is done; most of what is left is the kernels' reference reads (`CopyRows` 6% of the time for one load and store a row) |
| 5 | MV bookkeeping | 8.8 / 10.2 / 10.6% | 3.3 to 4.4 | 9 to 11% | ? | Two neighbour-context designs tried and dropped (2026-10-28, 2026-11-04), and the edge ctxIdxInc reads (2026-11-06) |
| 6 | Residual parsing, CABAC | 25.5% | 2.3 | – | small | `Cabac.ResidualBlock` is tight (engine in locals, contexts unchecked); the excess is in its share of a frame, not its code |
| 7 | Per-picture clears | 2.3 / 1.7 / 2.0% | 9.3 | 2% | 1% | Left before (each path would clear its own macroblock instead) |

Recommended next: **1**, the residual add in vectors (the whole 4x4 or 8x8 block's add and clip at once, DC-only
blocks as one broadcast add), checked against the scalar add as the transforms are now; and **2** as a question for
the owner, since it is the largest single item that is not code speed.
