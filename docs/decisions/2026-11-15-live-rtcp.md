# Live camera viewing, step 7: RTCP, receiver reports and the camera's clock (2026-11-15)

A plan, decided on by the owner (Decided, below). Issue #527, split from #462. Steps 2 to 6
(docs/decisions/2026-11-10-live-rtsp-h264.md to 2026-11-15-live-rtsps.md) read the camera's RTCP and ignore it, and send
none but the empty receiver report that opens a NAT (step 4, decision 3: "none at first"; step 5, decision 3: no lip
sync). Two of #527's leftovers follow from RTCP: receiver reports, and lip sync by sender reports.

## What RTCP gives

- **Receiver reports** (RFC 3550, 6.4.2) tell the sender what arrived: the stream's SSRC, the highest sequence number
  seen, the packets lost in all and as a fraction of those since the last report, the interarrival jitter, and the last
  sender report echoed (LSR) with the time since it came (DLSR), from which the sender works out the round trip. A
  compound packet starts with the report and carries a source description with a CNAME (6.1).
- **Why a camera wants them.** From memory, not measured: some servers take RTCP for the sign that a reader is still
  there and drop one they hear nothing from (live555-based ones count RTCP as liveness beside RTSP requests, and some
  cameras' RTSP stacks don't take `GET_PARAMETER` or `OPTIONS` as keep-alives), and FFmpeg's, VLC's and live555's
  clients all send them. mediamtx needs none (step 4, Measured). No camera was in reach to try.
- **Sender reports** (6.4.1) tie a track's RTP timestamps to the camera's wall clock: an NTP time and the RTP timestamp
  of the same instant. The two tracks' RTP timestamps have separate origins, so nothing else says which sound belongs
  to which picture.

## Proposal

1. **Receiver reports, always** (decision 1): every RTSP session sends one a track every 5 seconds (6.2's minimum
   interval, fixed: with one receiver there is nothing to spread out), from the session's PLAY until it ends.
   - Over the RTSP connection: interleaved on the channel after the track's (1, and 3 for the audio).
   - Over UDP: from the track's RTCP port to the server's (`server_port`'s second, else the one after its RTP port). A
     SETUP answer with no `server_port` gets none, as it gets no punch packets.
   - Each track is a receiver of its own: a random SSRC and a random CNAME of the session's (RFC 7022), nothing of the
     machine's.
   - Counted as packets arrive, before the reorder window: a duplicate counts as received and a late packet as
     arrived, as A.3 has it. `LiveStatistics.LostPackets` stays the depacketizers' count, after the window.
   - A stream whose SSRC changes (an encoder restarted) is counted from there.
   - A report that can't be sent ends nothing: the read of the connection sees the same failure.
2. **Sender reports** are read on both transports (the channel after the track's, the RTCP port): the last one's NTP
   time is echoed, while the stream's packets are from the SSRC that sent it. One may come before the stream's first
   packet.
3. **Lip sync by capture times** (decision 2), in a pull request of its own: RTSP frames and audio chunks gain a time on
   the camera's clock, from the track's last sender report and the RTP timestamp, null until that track has one. The
   player holds nothing back, so each still comes as soon as it is decoded: the caller, who knows how long its sound
   output buffers, delays whichever is early.
4. **Not in step 7**: RTCP BYE (a session's end is the connection's), reports' loss and jitter in `LiveStatistics`,
   feedback messages (RFC 4585: NACK, PLI), and randomizing the interval.

## Checks

- **`RtcpSessionTests`**, against RFC 3550's definitions on hand-made packets: A.3's loss counts (across the sequence
  number's wrap, reordering, duplicates making it negative, an interval's fraction), 6.4.1's jitter against its formula
  in floating point on random arrivals, LSR and DLSR, a sender report before the first packet, malformed RTCP, and a
  new SSRC.
- **`LiveRtcpTests`** through `LivePlayer` and `RtspTestServer`, on the test's clock: nothing before 5 seconds, then a
  report a track on channels 1 and 3 with the server's sender reports echoed; over UDP from the RTCP port, with a
  packet the server never sent counted lost.
- **Mutation entries** for the rules only some streams reach: the fraction never negative, and another stream's sender
  report not echoed; and for the UDP reports being sent at all.
- **Not checked:** a camera or server that needs them. mediamtx plays with and without.

## Decided

Asked with the multiple-choice prompt; the owner took each recommendation (2026-11-15).

1. **When receiver reports are sent. Decided: (a), always.**
   - **(a) Always** (recommended). What every other client does. No public API. A camera that drops silent readers
     just works, for about 60 bytes every 5 s a track.
   - (b) Opt-in, `RtspSource.ReceiverReports`. Sessions stay what 0.17 sends, but a camera that needs them fails until
     the user finds the switch.
   - (c) Opt-out: on, with a switch for a camera that mishandles them. None such has been seen.
2. **What the camera's clock is used for. Decided: (a), capture times on frames and chunks.**
   - **(a) Capture times** (recommended). No latency added, and the caller decides, knowing its output's delay.
   - (b) Opt-in sync in the player: `TakeLatest` returns the newest picture not ahead of the audio taken. No caller
     code, but latency for the picture, and the output device's own delay is ignored.
   - (c) Both.

## Since

- Built (2026-11-15): `RtcpSession` (internal, Playback), the reports' sending and the sender reports' reading in
  `LivePlayer`, `RtspTestServer` reading what a client interleaves and sending RTCP over UDP.
- Found by the checks: a sender report that came before the stream's first packet was forgotten when that packet
  started the count. Over UDP the two race, so it is kept, by its SSRC.
- Built (2026-11-15), point 3, in its own pull request: `RgbaFrame.CaptureTime` and `AudioChunk.CaptureTime`
  (`DateTime?`, UTC), from `RtcpSession.CaptureTime`: the sender report's NTP time and the distance of the RTP
  timestamp from the report's, either side, by the track's clock rate.
  - **H.264 with B pictures:** a unit's frame is not its picture (B pictures are sent before pictures shown ahead of
    them), and `H264Decoder` gave an Annex B stream's frames FFmpeg's times for a stream that has only its decoding
    order. `LivePlayer`'s decoder now gets each unit's RTP timestamp as its time and keeps it on the frame
    (`H264Decoder.PacketTimes`, internal). Dealing the timestamps out in order to the frames as they came was tried
    first: one frame more or fewer from the decoder than pictures given (a repeated picture, a gap) then shifted every
    later time, and an IDR picture couldn't put it right by counting.
  - **AAC with several frames a packet** (FFmpeg's packetizer): the packet has the first frame's timestamp, and the
    frames after it are placed by the length of those decoded before them, so a frame the decoder rejects moves the
    rest of its packet a frame early.
  - A sender report with an NTP time of zero (a camera with no clock) gives times from 1900: still one clock for both
    tracks. NTP's wrap in 2036 is read as the era after.
  - The samples (`mclive`, `mclive-wpf`, `mcplay`) don't use the times yet: issue #526's.
  - Checks: `RtcpSessionTests` (the mapping, both sides of the report, the wraps, another SSRC's report);
    `LiveRtcpTests` (both tracks on the camera's clock, null before the reports, B pictures from a new recording,
    `h264-b-motion.rtp`, a picture sent twice, AAC's later frames). Four mutation entries.
  - Not checked: a camera's own sender reports (their accuracy between tracks is the camera's), and how well the two
    line up when heard and seen.
