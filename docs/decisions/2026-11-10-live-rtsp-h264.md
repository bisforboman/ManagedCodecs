# Live camera viewing, step 2: RTSP with H.264 (2026-11-10)

A plan, decided on by the owner (below). Nothing here is built yet. It is step 2 of #462, after MJPEG over HTTP
(docs/decisions/2026-11-10-live-mjpeg-over-http.md, built). The survey on #462 found RTSP with H.264 to be what people
ask for most, at about five times MJPEG's count: almost every current IP camera serves it, and it is what
LibVLC or FFmpeg gets pulled in for.

## What a camera speaks

- **RTSP 1.0** (RFC 2326) is the session: `OPTIONS`, `DESCRIBE` (answered with SDP, RFC 4566), `SETUP` per track,
  `PLAY`, and `TEARDOWN`. Keep-alives (`GET_PARAMETER` or `OPTIONS`) go out before the `Session` header's timeout
  (60 s by default). Control URLs come from `Content-Base` and each track's `a=control`, relative or absolute. RTSP
  2.0 (RFC 7826) is rare in cameras and is out.
- **Authentication**: almost every camera answers `DESCRIBE` with 401. Some want Basic, most Digest (RFC 2617, MD5;
  `qop=auth` on some). Credentials come from the URL's user info (`rtsp://user:pass@host/...`), the form camera vendors
  document, or from a `NetworkCredential`.
- **Transport**: RTP over the RTSP TCP connection itself, interleaved (RFC 2326 §10.12: `$`, a channel byte, a 16-bit
  length, then the packet). It needs one connection and no UDP ports, and works through NAT and firewalls. It never
  loses packets, but a camera under load can still drop them before sending, which RTP sequence numbers show. UDP
  (two ports per track, loss, reordering) comes later.
- **H.264 over RTP** (RFC 6184), non-interleaved mode (`packetization-mode` 0 or 1). Each RTP packet is one of:
  - a single NAL unit (types 1 to 23);
  - STAP-A (24): several NAL units, each behind a 16-bit size;
  - FU-A (28): one NAL unit cut into fragments, the first and last marked.
  The interleaved mode (2: STAP-B, MTAP, FU-B) is for broadcasting and is out. An access unit is the packets with one
  RTP timestamp (90 kHz); the marker bit ends it. `sprop-parameter-sets` (base64 SPS and PPS) in the SDP's
  `a=fmtp`, when present, go to the decoder before the first picture.
- **Measured** with FFmpeg 6.1's packetizer (`-f rtp`, libx264, 2026-11-10):
  - 720p25: one STAP-A (the SPS, PPS and SEI), then FU-A for every slice.
  - 320x240 with 4 slices: STAP-A for the small slices, single NAL units and FU-A.
  - The SDP has `packetization-mode=1` and no `sprop-parameter-sets`: the parameter sets come in-band.
  So FFmpeg's RTP covers all three packet types. Hand-built packets cover the rest: `sprop-parameter-sets`, the
  boundary cases, damage.

## What the decoder already gives

- `H264Decoder` reads Annex B when it has no avcC (AVI's H.264, raw files). A depacketizer that puts a start code
  before each NAL unit and hands over one access unit a call needs no new decoder API.
- `ConcealErrors` hides a picture with missing slices and a missing picture (a gap in `frame_num`) behind copies from
  a reference, and marks frames `IsDamaged` until the next IDR picture. RTP's losses are exactly that.
- Frame threading (`Threads`) adds a frame of latency for each thread. A live view wants 1 by default; more only
  where one thread can't keep up.
- Annex B has no presentation times: the decoder numbers frames by decoding order, delayed by the reorder depth.
  Cameras rarely send B pictures. For a live view the time a frame arrived matters, as with MJPEG, not its RTP
  timestamp, so the frames keep the arrival time of their access unit.

## Proposal

1. **`RtspSource`** (Playback), the counterpart of `MjpegHttpSource`: the `rtsp://` URL (its user info as
   credentials), an optional `NetworkCredential`, `StallTimeout` (no RTP for 5 s), and the first video track in the
   SDP whose `rtpmap` encoding is H264. `LivePlayer` gains a constructor taking it. The reconnect rules stay as for
   MJPEG: back off 0.5 s, doubling to 10 s, starting again at 0.5 s after a frame; final on 401 after both auth
   schemes, 403 and 404; also final on 454 Session Not Found during `PLAY` and on an SDP with no H.264 track.
2. **The session layer, our own**: a minimal RTSP 1.0 client, RTP over TCP interleaved only:
   - `OPTIONS`, `DESCRIBE`, `SETUP`, `PLAY`, `GET_PARAMETER` keep-alives, `TEARDOWN`;
   - Basic and Digest, the SDP lines above, and `$`-framed RTP and RTCP (RTCP read and ignored; no receiver reports
     at first, which every camera tried so far must tolerate, to be confirmed).
   It is about a few hundred lines, from the RFCs, with no dependencies, compiled by the Unity build like the rest
   (decision 1 has the alternative).
3. **`RtpH264Depacketizer`** (internal): RTP packets in, an Annex B access unit out.
   - It reorders nothing over TCP. It notes sequence-number gaps and drops a NAL unit whose FU-A fragments aren't
     all there, but still passes the access unit's other NAL units on, so `ConcealErrors` hides the hole.
   - A decoder made with `ConcealErrors` on is always used for RTSP.
   - Each damaged frame and each gap is counted (`LiveStatistics.DamagedFrames`, plus `LostPackets`, new).
4. **Falling behind**: H.264 can't skip decoding a picture other pictures refer to, so the newest-wins slot works after
   decoding (conversion is skipped for a frame replaced before it was taken). If decoding falls behind the camera
   (a 4K stream on a small CPU), access units queue. Once they hold more than `MaxLag` (1 s by default, by arrival
   times), everything up to the next IDR picture is dropped (counted as `SkippedFrames`) and decoding starts again
   there. Never drop silently and never fall further behind (decision 3).
5. **Not in step 2**: RTP over UDP, RTSPS (TLS), RTSP over HTTP tunnelling, H.265, RFC 2435 JPEG over RTP, RFC 3640 AAC
   (the live player has no audio), ONVIF discovery and PTZ. JPEG over RTP is the likeliest next one: a few hundred
   lines more, rebuilding a JPEG's headers from RTP's 8-byte one and its tables for `MjpegDecoder`.

## Checks

- **The depacketizer against FFmpeg's packetizer**: `make-samples.sh` records FFmpeg's RTP for a few of the H.264
  samples, among them a sliced one and a 720p one, which is all three packet types (a recorder script, as for the
  other rewritten streams). A test checks that depacketizing gives back the sample's access units, byte for byte.
  The frames are then `H264Decoder`'s own, already checked against FFmpeg, so there is no second oracle.
- **Hand-built packets** cover `sprop-parameter-sets`, an FU-A missing its start, middle or end, a sequence-number
  wrap at 65535, a STAP-A with a zero-size unit, and `packetization-mode` 2 (refused).
- **The session against a loopback RTSP server** in the tests (no external server), with a fake clock as for MJPEG. It
  serves the recorded RTP interleaved and covers:
  - the 401 to Digest round trip, checked against RFC 2617's worked example, and Basic;
  - relative and absolute control URLs, keep-alives before the timeout, and a `Session` with `;timeout=`;
  - a stall, a dropped connection, and 454;
  - dropped RTP packets, giving damaged frames with no exception and the next IDR picture whole again;
  - falling behind, with a decoder slowed by a test hook, giving the jump to the next IDR picture.
  Each guard gets a mutation entry.
- **Real devices**, by hand: mediamtx (an RTSP server, serving FFmpeg's push) and an IP camera if one is reachable,
  listed in docs/conformance.md with what they showed. mediamtx in CI is decision 4.
- **Security analyzers**: Digest needs MD5, which CA5351 flags. Suppress it where it is used, with the justification
  that RFC 2617 requires it and the hash protects nothing of the user's
  (docs/decisions/2026-11-06-security-analyzers.md).

## Decided

Asked with the multiple-choice prompt; the owner took each proposal (2026-11-10).

1. **The session layer. Decided:** our own minimal RTSP 1.0 client (proposal 2), from RFC 2326 and RFC 2617, with no
   dependencies and compiled by the Unity build. SharpRTSP was the alternative: its three package dependencies, no
   AOT declaration, and no way into the Unity build's single assembly.
2. **The API. Decided:** `RtspSource` and a second `LivePlayer` constructor, adding only. An abstract `LiveSource` base
   was the alternative.
3. **Falling behind. Decided:** past `MaxLag` (1 s by default) of queued access units, drop everything up to the next
   IDR picture, count it as `SkippedFrames`, and decode on from there (proposal 4). Decoding everything was the
   alternative.
4. **A real RTSP server in CI. Decided:** nightly only. mediamtx, fed by FFmpeg, runs in `realworld.yml`; the required
   checks use the loopback server.
5. **Scope. Decided:** step 2 is H.264 over RTP interleaved on the RTSP connection. JPEG over RTP (RFC 2435), UDP and
   RTSPS are later steps.

A public API change, so a new minor version, with a CHANGELOG line under "Unreleased" when it is built.
